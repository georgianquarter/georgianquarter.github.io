# Our Quarter

Liverpool's Georgian Quarter is one of the city's most
distinctive historic neighbourhoods. Its elegant terraces,
broad streets, squares, churches and public buildings
reflect a period of extraordinary growth in Liverpool's
history.

Today, the Quarter is both an important part of the city's
architectural heritage and a living residential community.

## Where is the Georgian Quarter?

The Georgian Quarter, sometimes known as the Canning
Georgian Quarter, occupies an area immediately southeast
of Liverpool city centre.

Broadly, it extends from Gambier Terrace and Roscoe Street
in the west towards Falkner Street in the east, and from
Upper Parliament Street in the south towards Mount
Pleasant and Oxford Street in the north.

These are approximate neighbourhood boundaries rather
than a formal planning designation.

The Quarter includes some of Liverpool's best-known
historic streets and public spaces, but also quieter
residential streets that contribute just as much to its
distinctive character.

## A brief history

Liverpool's remarkable growth during the eighteenth
and nineteenth centuries transformed the city.

As the port expanded, new residential neighbourhoods
developed beyond the older commercial centre. The
higher ground to the southeast became an attractive
location for merchants, professionals and other
prosperous residents.

The development of the Georgian Quarter took place
over several decades. Its streets, terraces, churches
and squares reflect successive phases of urban
expansion rather than a single architectural plan.

During the twentieth century, the area experienced
substantial social and economic change. Some historic
buildings fell into disrepair, while others found
new uses.

Conservation and regeneration have since helped
protect much of the Quarter's architectural heritage,
although maintaining its historic character remains
an ongoing responsibility.

## Streets and landmarks

### Hope Street

Hope Street is one of Liverpool's most celebrated
streets, connecting the city's two cathedrals and
passing numerous cultural and historic institutions.

### Rodney Street

Rodney Street is renowned for its Georgian terraces
and its historical associations with Liverpool's
medical and professional communities.

### Canning Street

Canning Street and the surrounding terraces form
an important part of the Quarter's residential
heartland, with historic houses and distinctive
streetscapes.

### Falkner Square

Falkner Square is an important green space surrounded
by nineteenth-century residential architecture.

### Gambier Terrace

Gambier Terrace, overlooking St James's Gardens,
is among the area's most recognisable historic
terraces.

## Architecture and conservation

The Georgian Quarter's significance lies not only
in individual listed buildings but also in the
character of its streets, squares and public spaces.

Building proportions, architectural details,
traditional materials, railings, boundary walls
and the relationships between neighbouring
properties all contribute to the area's identity.

Parts of the Quarter fall within designated
conservation areas, including Canning Street,
Rodney Street and Mount Pleasant.

These statutory designations have their own
boundaries and planning requirements. They should
not be confused with the broader geographical
identity of the Georgian Quarter.

## The Quarter today

The Georgian Quarter remains an active residential
neighbourhood close to Liverpool city centre.

Residents value its architecture, green spaces,
walkable streets and proximity to the city's
cultural institutions.

At the same time, the Quarter faces the practical
challenges common to many urban neighbourhoods:
maintaining historic buildings, protecting public
spaces, managing traffic, addressing antisocial
behaviour and ensuring that streets remain safe
and welcoming.

The future of the Georgian Quarter depends on
balancing conservation with the everyday needs
of the people who live here.
